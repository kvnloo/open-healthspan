# RFC 0005: Evaluate meaningful healthspan-adjacent outcomes
Status: DRAFT RESEARCH DESIGN, not an approved or registered study · Oct 19, 2026

Question: does voluntary small-group accountability increase sustained, individually appropriate healthy behaviors **and** functional wellbeing versus private-only tracking?

Candidate pilot: 2-week baseline → ~8-week prospective study with group-randomized control where feasible → 4–12-week follow-up, with ethics/privacy/clinical advisers, power analysis and preregistration before recruitment.

Primary candidate: prespecified difference in the proportion of explicitly completed goal opportunities, with sensitivity analyses for unknowns/dropouts and group clustering. This is a behavior measure, **not proof of longer life**. A separate validated physical function / fatigue / quality-of-life outcome (e.g. appropriate licensed PROMIS measure) should be a prespecified secondary or confirmatory endpoint. Consider objectively measured activity/sleep regularity, harms, burden, injury, accessibility and retention.

Long-term clinical outcomes (blood pressure, glycemia, cardiovascular fitness/functional strength) require appropriate measurement methods, time, consent and sample size. HRV, vendor readiness and “biological age” remain exploratory surrogates, never longevity proof. Publish disclosure-reviewed aggregates, not participants' pseudonymous biometric histories. Watch inequity, coercion, overtraining, social anxiety and attrition.

Go/no-go: no real recruitment, upload, efficacy statement or research export until independently reviewed. Personal utility can improve even if the social experiment fails; solo use must stay usable.
