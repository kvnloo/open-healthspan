      01 · How it works

      Plan, keep it, share seven fields.

      1. Plan your week

        Pick 1 to 7 goals that fit you: a walk, lights out earlier, a planned rest day. Rest counts. You don't need a wearable or a lab test.

      2. Score on your device

        Your score is done ÷ (done + missed), capped at 100%. It's computed on your device, and day-level data never leaves it.

      3. Share a code, if you want

        When the week ends, you can paste one code into your group chat. It carries exactly seven fields, and the board rejects any code with extra fields.

      schemaversion tag
      group_idwhich circle
      member_idHMAC of a device secret: stable in one group, unlinkable across groups
      aliasany name you pick
      weekISO week, no timestamps
      goals_planned1–7
      progress_bandrounded down to 5%
      neverheart rate, HRV, sleep, steps, GPS, labs, weight, "biological age"

      02 · Why not biomarkers

      Competing on your blood work rewards the people who were already ahead.

      Biomarker leaderboards are a great show. For a circle of friends, though, they need money, lab visits and sharing medical numbers. They also reward genetics and the wrong kind of push. A plan-keeping board doesn't have those problems.

      Biomarker leaderboards

      • Rank pace of aging or biological age
      • Need lab kits or wearables
      • Share medical numbers publicly
      • Hard to win if you start behind

      Open Healthspan

      • Ranks how well you kept your own plan
      • Works by hand, no device needed
      • Shares a 5% band and an alias, nothing else
      • A beginner's 4 of 4 counts the same as an athlete's

      There's no streak shame, no score above 100, and no reward for overtraining. Read the rules in RFC 0002.

      04 · For med students and researchers design draft

      Research data that's consented and structured from day one.

      Getting data out of a hospital record system means HIPAA review, a queue for data pulls and weeks of chart review to sort people into groups. Here, people opt in themselves, and every entry is already structured: an ISO week, 1–7 goals, each marked done, missed or unknown. RFC 0006 describes the research path.

      Our draft study (RFC 0005) asks one question. Does a small accountability group help people keep healthy habits better than tracking alone? It needs ethics review, preregistration and clinical advisers before anyone is recruited. That's work students can lead.

      An IRB still applies to any university study. Research opt-in will stay separate from social sharing.

      Questiongroup accountability vs private tracking
      Design2-week baseline → ~8 weeks, randomized by group → 4–12-week follow-up
      Primaryshare of goal opportunities completed
      Secondarya validated function or quality-of-life measure (e.g. PROMIS)
      Not claimedlonger life; "biological age"
      StatusHOLD, no recruitment until reviewed

      Honest limits

      • Pseudonymous, not anonymous. Your friends know who "Otter" is.
      • The share code is encoded, not encrypted. Anyone with the code can read its seven fields.
      • It measures plan-keeping, not health. It doesn't diagnose, treat or claim to extend life.
